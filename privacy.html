Privacy Policy

One Line Journaling · Effective 13 August 2026

One Line Journaling is a private journal. This page says, in plain words, what we keep and what we will never do with it.

What we store

Your email address (to sign you in), your first name, your settings (like your daily reminder), and your journal, one line and one color per day.

Usage counts

To decide what to improve, we may count which parts of the app get used, like how often sharing or search is opened. A count is only the button’s name, your account, and the time. It never includes your words or anything you typed. These counts are linked to your account only so they can be erased with it. They are never sold, never shared, and never used for ads.

Where it lives

Your journal is stored in our database (hosted on Supabase) and protected in transit and at rest with industry-standard encryption. Server access rules mean your account can only ever read its own lines. Your phone also keeps an offline copy so the app works without a connection.

What we will never do

We don’t sell your data. We don’t show ads. We don’t use third-party trackers or ad companies. Your words are yours.

Email, no spam ever

The only emails we send are your 6-digit sign-in code and replies when you contact us, including a confirmation exchange before we act on an emailed account request. If you email us about your account, we keep that correspondence and our notes about the request for up to 90 days after it’s resolved, then delete them. No newsletters, no marketing. If something important changes (like this policy), we’ll tell you inside the app.

Reminders & sharing

The daily reminder is scheduled on your phone itself, not sent from our servers. Share images are created on your phone and handed to your device’s share sheet; we never see or store what you share.

Deleting your account & data

In the app: Settings → Delete account & data immediately and permanently erases your account, your profile, and every line from our database, and removes the offline copy stored on this phone. If you also installed One Line on another phone, deleting the app there clears its offline copy too.

One honest detail: we also keep encrypted recovery copies of the journal database and persistent account and sign-in records, so a disaster has a recovery point instead of destroying the whole database. A deleted account can normally remain inside those recovery copies for up to two weeks. If making new copies stops, we keep the last recovery copy until a replacement is safely stored, so in that exceptional case it can be older than two weeks. We authenticate and open a copy only to verify that it works, run a controlled recovery test, or recover from a real failure. Automatic recovery builds a separate empty project; it does not overwrite the live database in place.

Already deleted the app? You can still have your account erased, no need to reinstall. Email us at onelinejournaling@gmail.com from your account’s email address and we’ll permanently erase your account and every line from our database within 30 days.

None of this contains an email, a name, or journal content. What remains after you delete is one small marker, so your other phones know to clear their own offline copies. The marker holds the account’s random ID, the time of deletion, and a scrambled check code from each phone that set one up. It answers that check for 60 days and is removed after that window. While you use the app, each signed-in phone keeps one check code, and our server keeps a scrambled form of it with your account’s random ID and the time it was registered. The server’s scrambled copy lives only as long as the account, or inside the marker for its 60 days; the code on a phone stays until that phone finishes its cleanup or the app is removed. While the account exists, a signed-in phone with an internet connection can hand its code back. A phone that is offline, stays unopened past the 60 days, or whose codes were reset by our support and not registered again before the deletion will not notice it; its offline copy then remains until the app is removed there.

Current provider details: Supabase Free gives us about one day of security and service logs and says it may maintain up to seven daily platform backup copies that become available if we upgrade. Brevo keeps sign-in delivery logs for one month, anonymizes future open/click events, and never stores the email-content preview. For account-support cases we use Gmail for the thread, a private Google Drive case file, and Google Calendar for the case reminders. On or before the case’s delete-by date (no later than 90 days after resolution), we permanently delete each individual thread, file, and reminder and do not leave those items in our trash. These providers may still retain limited security, legal, disaster-recovery, or anonymized information under their own policies.

Children

One Line Journaling is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Contact

Questions about your privacy? Email onelinejournaling@gmail.com.

If our practices change, we’ll update this page and the effective date above.
